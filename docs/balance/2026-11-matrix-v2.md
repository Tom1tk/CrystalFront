# Balance matrix 2026-11, v2 bots (G-10 M0 T10, P-6b)

**Status: awaiting the user's decision.** The per-map gate passes on 9 of 10 maps, but only because the two turtle bots beat each other. Against everything else, the "turtle, then grab nodes" plan still wins on 6 of 10 maps. T1 (`2026-11-matrix.md`) is the earlier run with the v1 bots.

## Setup
- Engine v0.8.6-ML, ENGINE_VERSION 13 (commit e2b5d42).
- `npx tsx headless/src/balance.ts --games 10 --bots v2:idle,v2:rush1,v2:rush2,v2:rush3,v2:turtle,v2:macro,v2:heavy,v2:hidden,v2:sw_rush_turtle,v2:sw_macro_bruiser,v2:sw_fake_brush,v2:sw_turtle_grab --maps all --maxTicks 12000 --jobs 12`
- 12 v2 scripted bots from T8 (`headless/src/v2/scripted.ts`), including the 4 that switch strategy mid-game (`sw_*`). Each bot picks its plan variant from its z.
- All 10 pool maps, 10 seeds per seat order, so **20 games per pairing per map**. 14,400 games in 5,638 s on 12 cores.
- Raw results (2 MB JSON, not committed): `/root/archive/balance/2026-11-matrix-v2.json`.
- Score: share of games won. **A timeout counts 0.5** (v2 games end with no winner at the cap; T1 counted the engine's tiebreak winner instead).
- 20 games per cell gives a 95% interval of about ±0.2, so single per-map cells are noisy. The overall table pools 200 games per cell (±0.07).

## Flags
- **Timeouts: 23% of all games (T1: 6%).** Every mirror pairing is 86–100% timeouts, and so are many weak-v-weak pairings (rush1 v sw_fake_brush 83%, hidden v rush1 78%). By map: 15% (basalt-run) to 33% (twin-spires). The rush bots' late economy rebuild (T8) keeps a beaten rush alive without the army to finish anyone. This matters for training cost: capped episodes are the longest.
- **Mirror seat share:** 0.50–0.51 on every map (1,200 mirror games), and 0.500 over all non-mirror games. No seat advantage. This confirms the T6/rotate symmetry fixes.
- **Win types:** combat 69%, timeout 23%, control 8% (T1: control 36%). Turtle now mostly wins by combat (1,397 combat, 114 control wins against non-idle bots).
- **Game length (decided games):** mean 5,633, median 5,396, max 11,936 ticks (T1: mean 3,822).
- **Weak bots:** rush1 (0.30), rush2 (0.29), sw_fake_brush (0.25) and hidden (0.33) are well below the field. They are useful as easy opponents for early training, but they don't pressure anyone.

## Reading
The v2 bots changed the picture without changing the conclusion. There is now some rock-paper-scissors: rush3 beats macro-style bots on some maps, `sw_rush_turtle` holds turtle, and macro wins hollow-crown and twin-spires. But the single strongest plan is still defensive. Build turrets and gunners behind them, survive the first push, then walk out and take nodes. `sw_turtle_grab` averages 0.88 against the field and loses to nothing but turtle (0.33) overall. A learner trained against this pool, and later itself, would probably converge on it on 6–7 of 10 maps.

The two exceptions are hollow-crown and twin-spires, where a mixed army that contests nodes early beats the turtle. I haven't yet checked which map features cause this; the lever sweep below would test the obvious ones. On the other maps, turtling seems to cost nothing because the nodes can still be taken late.

## Options (for the user to decide)
1. **(Recommended) Run a lever sweep against the v2 bots, then decide.** Offline only; no game change is committed without your OK. Test 3–4 candidates on 7 bots (turtle, sw_turtle_grab, macro, heavy, rush3, sw_rush_turtle, sw_macro_bruiser) × 10 maps × 20 games (~35 min each on 18 cores). Candidates:
   - turret range 150 → 120, so turrets no longer outrange gunners;
   - turret cost 50 → 75, or turret build time up, so turtling has a real economic cost;
   - node income or early node bonus up, so the side that leaves home early gets ahead;
   - `CONTROL.holdTicks` down (T1 showed longer holds favour turtle).
   I'd bring back the table and one recommended change, which would need an ENGINE_VERSION bump.
2. **Accept the balance and start M1 (T11).** M1 trains against scripted opponents only, so the turtle bias shows up later, at self-play (M2). The risk is spending M1/M2 compute on a meta we then change, which would mean retraining.
3. **Fix the timeouts first** (bot-side: give rush bots a finish-or-turtle fallback). This is independent of 1 or 2 and doesn't change the game.

## Lever sweep (option 1, run 2026-11-12)
7 bots (turtle, sw_turtle_grab, macro, heavy, rush3, sw_rush_turtle, sw_macro_bruiser) × 10 maps × 10 seeds per side, 4,900 games per lever, about 29 min each. Overrides applied at runtime only; nothing was committed. Raw data and scripts: `/root/archive/balance/sweep-2026-11-12/`.

| lever | turtle_grab v non-turtle | turtle v non-turtle | macro v field | maps where turtle_grab >0.80 v every non-turtle | timeouts (non-mirror) | control wins |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base (no change) | 0.86 | 0.81 | 0.49 | 6/10 | 14% | 8% |
| turret range 150 → 120 | 0.85 | 0.77 | **0.55** | 5/10 | 13% | 9% |

| targeting: threat order | 0.86 | 0.80 | 0.51 | 6/10 | 15% | 8% |
| targeting: units first | 0.87 | 0.80 | 0.51 | 6/10 | 15% | 8% |

**Reading:** no lever breaks the turtle lead. Even doubling the turret cost leaves `sw_turtle_grab` at 0.90 against non-turtle bots (it gets *stronger*, since the opponents' turrets get dearer too). So the dominance isn't mainly turret stats. The likelier cause is how the other bots fight: one army order per 0.8 s, attack-moving into turrets with no focus fire, no healing, no repair, no guard, and targets that get dragged across the map. Those are exactly the gaps G-13 (`docs/BOT_CONTROL_PLAN.md`) closes.

**Decision:** no stat change now. Implement G-13 Phase E, then at E6 re-run the matrix with E plus the strongest small lever (turret range 120, the only one that lifted macro noticeably), and bring both to the user.

**Reading:**
- Phase E alone hardly moves the balance: −0.02 for turtle_grab, but +0.04 for the plain turtle.
- Turret range 120 again helps macro most (+0.05) and takes one map off the dominant list. It is still the only lever that does anything.
- The v3 bots' new micro helps the defending side more: turtles gain 0.04 and macro loses 0.03. Guard, focus and heal pay off most when fighting next to your own turrets, and the attackers' scripted micro is crude.
- So the turtle lead now looks like a combination of cheap, strong static defence and attack logic that is too simple. The scripted bots are hand-written, not tuned, so their skill is a weak measure of the game's balance.

**Decision (user, 2026-11-12):** turret range 120 adopted, and repair now needs range (v0.8.10-ML, ENGINE_VERSION 15). Scripted bots now also repair turrets, which favours turtles somewhat; botCheck after the change: turtle 41/1/8, sw_turtle_grab 44/4/2, macro 37/7/6 (as blue, W/L/T of 50).
